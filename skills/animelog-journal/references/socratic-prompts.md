# Socratic Reflection Framework for Anime & Literature

A journal is only as valuable as the honesty of its reflections. Rather than asking *"Did you like this show?"*, use these Socratic inquiry templates based on genre and narrative theme to help the user articulate enduring life lessons.

---

## 1. The Core 3-Question Post-Title Method

Use this whenever a user finishes an anime series, movie, or book:

1. **The Crux Dilemma:** *"What was the pivotal moment where a character had to sacrifice something they wanted for something they believed in?"*
2. **The Mirror Question:** *"Where in your own life have you faced a similar dilemma—or where are you currently avoiding a hard choice?"*
3. **The Eternal Maxim:** *"If you had to summarize the moral or philosophical lesson of this journey in one sentence for your future self, what is it?"*

---

## 2. Genre-Specific Reflection Prompts

### A. Seinen & Historical Fiction (e.g. *Vinland Saga*, *Vagabond*, *Monster*)
- **Theme:** True strength, forgiveness, the cycle of violence, and redemption.
- **Questions:**
  - *"Thors says 'You have no enemies.' Who in your life do you currently treat as an adversary, and what would happen if you ceased viewing them as an opponent?"*
  - *"How did the protagonist's definition of victory change between the beginning and the end?"*
  - *"What was the price paid for revenge or obsession?"*

### B. High Fantasy & Mortality (e.g. *Frieren: Beyond Journey's End*, *To Your Eternity*)
- **Theme:** Transience of time, memory, grief, and unhurried presence.
- **Questions:**
  - *"Frieren realizes too late that Himmel's 10 years with her were precious. What routine conversation or relationship in your day-to-day life are you taking for granted?"*
  - *"How does awareness of death change how you want to spend this week?"*

### C. Psychological & Identity (e.g. *Steins;Gate*, *Evangelion*, *Lain*, *Psycho-Pass*)
- **Theme:** Responsibility, loneliness, free will vs determinism, and technological hubris.
- **Questions:**
  - *"Okabe attempts to shoulder the weight of all timelines alone before learning to trust others. Where are you trying to be a solitary hero instead of letting others help?"*
  - *"If a system promises safety in exchange for moral agency, is the trade worthwhile?"*

### D. Slice of Life & Craftsmanship (e.g. *A Place Further Than the Universe*, *Shirobako*, *Blue Period*)
- **Theme:** Ambition, impostor syndrome, creative discipline, and youth.
- **Questions:**
  - *"What gave the characters the courage to start before they felt ready?"*
  - *"How do they handle the gap between their taste and their current technical skill?"*

---

## 3. The "Rewatch Perspective Diff" Framework

When logging a second or third pass:
1. **The Age Diff:** *"How old were you when you first experienced this story? What has real life taught you since then that made you see this differently?"*
2. **The Antagonist Shift:** *"Did you find yourself sympathizing with an adult or antagonist you originally dismissed as unreasonable when you were younger?"*
3. **The Subtext:** *"What quiet detail or foreshadowing hit you hardest now that you already knew the ending?"*

---

## 4. Character Virtue Extraction

When saving a Favorite Character:
- **Virtue Archetype:** Identify the singular ideal they embody (e.g., Unconditional Kindness, Moral Courage, Pragmatic Duty, Quiet Perseverance).
- **The Human Flaw:** Note why their virtue feels earned rather than unrealistic (their vulnerability or struggle).
- **The Formula:**
  > `"[Character Name] exemplifies [Core Virtue] through [Key Scene/Choice]. They remind me that [Real-world Application]."`
